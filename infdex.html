India Payroll — Definitions, Formulas & Applicability

1. Payroll fundamentals

Payroll

The process of calculating an employee's payable salary for a pay period, applying attendance/leave/proration, earnings, statutory contributions, deductions, taxes and producing the final payslip/payable amount.

Pay Period

The period for which salary is processed, normally monthly. Example: 1–30 September.

Salary / Compensation

Money and benefits provided to an employee for employment. A payroll system normally separates recurring earnings, variable earnings, employer benefits and employee deductions.

CTC

Cost to Company: the employer's annualized cost of employing the person. CTC is not the same as gross salary or take-home pay.

CTC = Gross Earnings + Employer Statutory Contributions + Employer Benefits/Other CTC Components

Gross Salary / Gross Earnings

Total employee earnings before employee-side deductions such as PF, ESI, PT, TDS and recoveries.

Gross = Basic + DA + HRA + Allowances + Incentives/Bonus + Overtime + Other Earnings

Net Pay / Take-home

The amount actually payable to the employee after employee deductions.

Net Pay = Gross Earnings − Total Employee Deductions
Important: CTC, Gross, Gross Wage, PF Wage, ESI Wage, Taxable Salary and Net Pay are different bases. Never use one as a substitute for another.

2. Code on Wages — “full wage code” / 50% wage rule

The Code on Wages, 2019 defines “wages” broadly. It expressly includes basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, while excluding specified items such as HRA, conveyance allowance, certain special-expense payments, overtime, commission, gratuity on termination and certain other components, subject to the statutory conditions.

The Code also contains an important 50% rule: where the specified excluded components under the proviso exceed one-half (or another percentage notified by the Central Government) of total remuneration calculated under the provision, the excess is added back to wages.

Code-on-Wages basis (simplified) = Basic + DA + Retaining Allowance + Add-back required under the 50% rule, where applicable
Do not interpret “50%” as “Basic must always be exactly 50% of CTC”. The statutory wage definition and the add-back mechanism determine wages; employer PF/pension and other statutory components also have specific treatment. Payroll implementation should follow the applicable Central/State rules and current labour-code notifications.

Basic salary — how is it calculated?

There is no single universal payroll formula requiring Basic to be a fixed percentage of CTC for every employee. It is normally configured by the employer's salary policy and employment terms.

Example structure: Basic = 40% × Annual Gross Salary Monthly Basic = Annual Basic ÷ 12

Another employer may use 50% of gross, a fixed amount, or another salary-design method. The statutory wage definition should not be confused with a company's internal Basic-pay formula.

5. PF / EPF

What is PF?

EPF is the Employees' Provident Fund system administered by EPFO. The employee and employer make statutory contributions subject to the applicable EPF/EPS/EDLI rules.

Restricted PF — what does it mean?

“Restricted PF” is commonly a payroll-system term, not the name of a separate PF scheme. It normally means the PF contribution is calculated on the statutory wage ceiling rather than the employee's full PF wage.

If PF wage = ₹40,000 and the applicable PF contribution base is restricted to ₹15,000: Employee PF = 12% × ₹15,000 = ₹1,800

EPFO's published contribution material states that contributions are payable on a maximum wage ceiling of ₹15,000 for ordinary cases, while higher-wage contribution can be made under the applicable rules/requirements. International workers have separate treatment.

Typical PF calculation

ItemTypical rate/base
Employee EPF12% of applicable PF wage (10% for specified establishments/categories under the EPF rules)
Employer shareGenerally 12% total employer statutory contribution, with the prescribed portion diverted to EPS where applicable.
EPSEmployer-side pension contribution; no separate employee EPS contribution.
EDLIEmployer-side insurance contribution under applicable EPF rules.
Employee PF = PF Wage × Employee PF Rate
Employer PF = Employer EPF/EPS allocation according to applicable EPF rules
Do not calculate EPS, EDLI and employer PF as if all are additional 12% employee deductions. They are separate statutory components.

6. ESI / ESIC

What is ESI?

Employees' State Insurance is a social-security scheme administered by ESIC. It provides specified medical, sickness, maternity, disablement, dependants' and other benefits to covered persons subject to the scheme.

When does ESI apply?

Coverage depends on the establishment being covered and the employee satisfying the wage/eligibility conditions under the ESI law and current notifications. A commonly used wage ceiling for coverage is ₹21,000 per month (₹25,000 for persons with disability), subject to the statutory rules.

Employee ESI = ESI Wages × 0.75%
Employer ESI = ESI Wages × 3.25%

ESIC's published material confirms the current contribution split of 0.75% employee and 3.25% employer.

Important: ESI coverage is not simply “gross salary below ₹21,000 = ESI” in every situation. Establishment coverage, wage definition, contribution period and special rules must also be checked.

7. Income-tax / TDS from salary

TDS is the employer's periodic deduction of estimated income tax from salary. The annual tax calculation depends on the employee's tax regime, taxable salary, eligible deductions/exemptions, other income declared for TDS and applicable rates/rebates/cess.

Core calculation

Annual Taxable Income = Salary/other taxable income − deductions/exemptions allowed under the selected tax regime
Annual Tax Liability = Slab Tax + Surcharge (if applicable) + Health & Education Cess − eligible rebate/relief
Monthly TDS ≈ Remaining Annual Tax Liability ÷ Remaining Salary Months

Actual payroll TDS can differ because of declarations, previous-employer income, bonus timing, investment proofs, regime selection and year-end true-up.

Attendance / LOP

Payroll systems may use calendar days, fixed 30 days, payable days or working days depending on company policy. The denominator must be configured explicitly.

Payable Days = Eligible Days − LOP Days
Prorated Salary = Monthly Salary × Payable Days ÷ Proration Denominator

Overtime

Overtime must follow the applicable labour law and establishment rules. A generic payroll-engine formula is:

OT Pay = Eligible OT Hours × OT Hourly Rate × OT Multiplier

Do not hard-code a multiplier without confirming the applicable law/policy.

9. Worked examples

Example A — simple monthly payroll

EarningAmount
Basic₹30,000
HRA₹15,000
Special Allowance₹10,000
Gross₹55,000

If employee PF is restricted to ₹15,000:

PF = ₹15,000 × 12% = ₹1,800

If PT is ₹200 and TDS is ₹3,000 for the month:

Net Pay = ₹55,000 − ₹1,800 − ₹200 − ₹3,000 = ₹50,000

Example B — proration

Monthly Basic = ₹30,000. Company uses 30-day proration. LOP = 2 days.

LOP = ₹30,000 × 2 ÷ 30 = ₹2,000 Payable Basic = ₹30,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹28,000

Example C — CTC vs gross vs take-home

Annual Gross = ₹7,20,000 Employer PF = ₹21,600 Employer ESI/benefits = ₹0 for this example Annual CTC = ₹7,41,600

The employee's take-home is still lower than gross because employee-side PF, tax, PT and other valid deductions are removed from gross.

10. Payroll processing flow

  1. Employee master: joining date, salary structure, PF/ESI status, tax regime, state, bank and statutory identifiers.
  2. Attendance: payable days, present days, leave, holidays, LOP and overtime.
  3. Salary structure: calculate Basic, DA, HRA, allowances and other earnings.
  4. Proration: apply joining, exit, LOP and other proration rules.
  5. Variable earnings: incentives, overtime, bonus, arrears, reimbursements and adjustments.
  6. Statutory bases: determine PF wage, ESI wage, tax salary and other statutory bases independently.
  7. Employee deductions: PF, ESI, PT, TDS, loans and valid recoveries.
  8. Employer contributions: PF/EPS/EDLI, ESI and other applicable employer costs.
  9. Net salary: gross minus employee deductions.
  10. Validation: negative net pay, statutory ceilings, contribution rounding, joining/exit dates, duplicate deductions, tax annualization.
  11. Outputs: payslip, bank transfer file, statutory reports/returns and accounting journals.

12. Important implementation rules

  • Keep salary component, statutory wage base and taxable base as separate concepts in your payroll database.
  • Do not assume Basic = 50% of CTC unless that is your configured salary policy and it produces the required statutory wage treatment.
  • Do not assume PF = 12% of gross. PF uses the applicable PF wage/base and statutory rules.
  • Do not assume ESI = 4% employee deduction. The employee share is 0.75%; employer share is 3.25%.
  • Do not assume CTC = Gross. Employer contributions and benefits may be part of CTC.
  • Do not assume Gross = taxable salary. Taxability is component-specific and regime-specific.
  • Keep statutory rates, ceilings, state PT slabs and tax rules configurable rather than hard-coded.
  • Round statutory amounts exactly according to the applicable statutory/reporting rules and your payroll specification.
